# Code of Conduct

## The short version

Be civil. Argue about the code, not the person. Assume the other party is trying to help until
you have real evidence otherwise.

## What is expected

- **Disagree about substance.** "This will break on a machine without a GPU, here's why" is
  exactly the kind of comment this project wants, however bluntly it is phrased.
- **Say what you actually tested.** Claims without evidence waste everyone's time, and this
  project applies that standard to its own README.
- **Write in whatever language you are comfortable with.** English and 简体中文 are both fine;
  other languages are welcome and will be dealt with via translation.

## What is not acceptable

- Personal attacks, harassment, or discriminatory language of any kind
- Publishing someone's private information without their permission
- Sustained disruption: reopening settled decisions without new arguments, or derailing threads
  onto unrelated topics
- Sexualised language or imagery, and unwelcome sexual attention

This applies in issues, pull requests, discussions, and commit messages.

## Enforcement

Report problems privately to the repository owner through
[GitHub Security Advisories](https://github.com/Aevorine/Synorive/security/advisories/new)
(it is a private channel, and abuse reports are welcome there even though it is labelled for
security) or by opening a minimal issue asking to be contacted.

Reports are handled by the maintainer. Responses range from a request to edit a comment, through a
temporary block, to a permanent ban, depending on severity and whether it is a repeat. There is no
committee and no appeals board — this is a small project, and pretending to have a formal process
would be worse than saying so.

Whoever handles a report will not act on an incident they are personally involved in without
asking someone uninvolved to look at it first.
